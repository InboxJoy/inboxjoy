# Trademark policy

The InboxJoy source code is open source under the
[GNU Affero General Public License v3.0](LICENSE). The InboxJoy **name and
logo are not**. They are trademarks of the InboxJoy maintainers and are not
covered by the AGPL. This page explains what that means in practice.

In short: you can run, change and fork the code freely. If you publish a
modified version or sell it as a service, give it your own name and logo.

This is a plain-English summary, not legal advice. If something here conflicts
with applicable law, the law wins.

## What the policy covers

- The name "InboxJoy" and close variants (for example "Inbox Joy", "InboxJoy
  Cloud", "InboxJoy Pro").
- The InboxJoy logo, app icon and wordmark, including the files in
  `apps/web/public/brand/` that show them.
- Our domain names, including `inboxjoy.online`.

Third-party logos in the repository (Gmail, Outlook, Claude, OpenAI) belong to
their owners and are used only to show compatibility.

## You may, without asking

- Self-host InboxJoy for yourself, your company or your clients, and say that
  you run InboxJoy.
- Write blog posts, tutorials, reviews and videos about InboxJoy.
- Say that your tool is "compatible with InboxJoy" or "built on the InboxJoy
  open-source code", as long as it is clearly true and does not imply
  endorsement.
- Link to our website and repository, and use the name as plain text to refer
  to the project.
- Keep the name and logo in your own private, unmodified self-hosted copy,
  including the footer and page titles.
- Use the name in a pull request, issue, fork description or discussion about
  the project.

## You may not

- Name your fork, product, company, domain, app-store listing or social account
  "InboxJoy" or anything confusingly similar.
- Use the InboxJoy logo or a modified version of it as the logo of your own
  product or service.
- Offer a modified version of InboxJoy to the public under the InboxJoy name,
  or in a way that suggests it is the official product or is run, endorsed or
  supported by us.
- Sell managed hosting, support or consulting using the InboxJoy name or logo
  as your own brand. Saying "we host the open-source InboxJoy" is fine if you
  also say clearly that you are independent of the maintainers.
- Register domains, handles or trademarks that contain "InboxJoy".
- Imply a partnership, certification or endorsement that does not exist.

## If you fork the project

Before you publish a fork or run it as a public service:

1. Choose a new product name and replace the InboxJoy name and logo in the web
   app (page titles, the sidebar, emails, the footer, `apps/web/public/brand/`,
   the Open Graph images and the package names if you wish).
2. Keep the copyright notices and the AGPL licence text.
3. Publish your modifications under the AGPL if users interact with your
   version over a network (section 13 of the licence). Link to your source
   from the app.
4. Say in your README that your project is based on InboxJoy and is not the
   official version. A line such as "Based on InboxJoy; not affiliated with the
   InboxJoy maintainers" is enough.
